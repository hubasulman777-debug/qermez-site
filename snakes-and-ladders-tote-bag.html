The story of the board

Snakes and Ladders began in the subcontinent as Moksha Patam, a game of virtue and vice. The ladders stood for good deeds that lift you up, the snakes for the habits that drag you back down, and the last square was the goal of the whole journey. It travelled to England in the 19th century and returned to us as Saanp Seerhi, the game every child knows.

Our Moksha Patam tote prints that old board on unbleached cotton canvas. Lay it flat, set out the pieces and the die, and play on the floor, the dastarkhwan or the back seat of a long drive.

A lovely gift for children and grown-ups alike, for families abroad, and for anyone who still remembers the joy and agony of that last snake before square 100.

Caring for your canvas

  • The bag is washable. Turn it inside out before washing.
  • Hand wash in cold water with a mild detergent. No bleach, no wringing, no tumble dryer.
  • For hand-painted Studies, spot clean where you can.
  • Don’t iron or press directly on the bag.
  • If the canvas shrinks a little after washing, lay it flat, place some weight on it and let it dry. It will settle back into shape.
  • Dry flat in the shade. Keep the game pieces dry and stored in their potli.

Every bag is made by hand, so small variations in ink and colour are part of its character, not defects.
